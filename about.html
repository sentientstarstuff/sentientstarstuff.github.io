Brief Formal Bio

Dr. Devontae Baxter is a Postdoctoral Fellow studying the impact of cosmic environment on galaxy formation and evolution. He completed his undergraduate studies at the Georgia Institute of Technology, majoring in Physics (Astrophysics specialization) with a minor in Spanish. He earned his PhD in Physics at UC Irvine under the supervision of Professor Michael Cooper, centered on merging machine learning, cosmological simulations, and multi-observatory datasets (SDSS, Keck, Gemini) to infer the physical mechanisms driving star-formation quenching in galaxy groups and clusters.

The Skateboarding Astrophysicist

Born and raised in Atlanta, Georgia, my twin passions for science and skateboarding ignited during my teenage years. Before aspiring to explore the cosmos, my dream was to travel the globe as a professional skateboarder.

While my career path shifted toward the stars, the grit, creativity, and persistence I learned on a skateboard continue to shape how I approach complex astrophysical problems. Today, I’m fortunate to utilize world-class telescopes while still finding time to skate.

After graduating from Stockbridge High School in 2011, I embarked on a self-discovery gap year working and reading at my local library. Immersing myself in books by Carl Sagan, Michio Kaku, Brian Cox, and Neil deGrasse Tyson sparked my calling to contribute to humanity's collective knowledge of the Universe.
